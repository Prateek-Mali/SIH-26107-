**1. Which standard applies to you**
The product falls under the “Potable Water Bottles (Steel)” category covered by the Quality Control Order (QCO) for Potable Water Bottles and the relevant Indian Standard (IS 17803:2022) [1].

**2. Is it compulsory**
Yes. Potable water bottles are listed as a product under compulsory certification (Scheme I – ISI mark) [1].

**4. Steps, in order**

| Step | Who does it | What you need to do | Time / notes |
|------|-------------|---------------------|--------------|
| 1. Check eligibility & gather information | **You** | Prepare a declaration that the entire production will be marked with the BIS mark and that you are not seeking BIS approval for any brand name or trademark [2]. | – |

**5. Documents, fees and timelines**

- **Product samples** (to be submitted within 30 days) [3].
- **Marking fee** (tentative fee proposed by the concerned BO before factory visit) [4].
- **Licence fee** (as per the fee schedule in the Scheme‑I guidelines). [4]

**6. Common mistakes to avoid**

- Not submitting samples within the 30‑day window, which can lead to rejection [3].
- Ignoring the requirement to mark every bottle with the BIS mark, regardless of brand name [2].
- Failing to take corrective actions or not providing fresh samples within 30 days after a suspension, which may result in licence cancellation [5].

**7. What to do today**

1. Visit the **Manak Online** portal and create an account [https://www.manakonline.in/].
2. Download the Scheme‑I application form and the declaration template (Annexure‑I).
3. Prepare your product specification according to **IS 17803:2022** and arrange for steel water‑bottle samples. [1]
4. Submit the completed application with the declaration and pay the initial processing fee.

## 2. Difference between ISI mark and CRS?

*intent=compare · role=manufacturer · goal: understand the difference between the ISI mark and the CRS · standalone: "What is the difference between the ISI mark and the CRS?" · answer by groq:openai/gpt-oss-120b (understand by groq:openai/gpt-oss-20b) · 28.3s · 5 sources · check: 2 removed, 5 re-cited*

**Comparison of ISI Mark vs. CRS (Compulsory Registration Scheme)**

| Aspect | ISI Mark (Scheme‑I) | CRS – Compulsory Registration (Scheme‑II) |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Applicable products** | General industrial, consumer and other goods covered under the ISI licence scheme (e.g., electrical appliances, mechanical items). | Electronics and IT goods (e.g., digital cameras, mobile phones, UPS, copying machines) that are listed in the “Compulsory Registration Scheme” notified by the Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology[1]. |
| **Legal basis** | ISI licence is granted under the BIS Act and the ISI licence scheme (Scheme‑I). | Registration is required under the *Electronics & Information Technology Goods (Requirements for Compulsory Registration) Order* (2012, 2021 and its 2026 amendment)[2][3]. |
| **Mark shown on product** | **ISI Mark** – a physical symbol indicating the product has a BIS licence. | **Standard Mark** (same “BIS Standard Mark” but issued under CRS). The mark can be displayed physically or electronically (e‑labelling) for devices with an integral display[4]. |
| **Validity of licence** | Initially 2 years, renewable up to 5 years[5]. | Licence validity is also 2 years initially and can be renewed for a further period not less than 2 years and up to 5 years[5]. |

### Which one fits you?
- If you are manufacturing **electronics or IT equipment** (e.g., digital cameras, mobile phones, UPS, copying machines) that appear in the compulsory registration list, you must obtain a **CRS registration** and use the **Standard Mark** (physical or e‑label)[2][3].
