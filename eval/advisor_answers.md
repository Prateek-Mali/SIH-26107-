**DEPENDS** – Importing LED bulbs from China requires compliance with the Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS) for Electronics & IT Goods, not the FMCS, because LED bulbs fall under the electronics category [1][2].

**Why:**
- LED bulbs (e.g., Self‑Ballasted LED Lamps, Fixed General Purpose LED Luminaries, LED Hand lamps, LED Lighting Chains) are listed under the CRS for electronics and IT goods [1][3][4][5].
- The CRS is a mandatory registration (self‑declaration) scheme for such products, mandated by the Electronics & Information Technology Goods (Requirement of Compulsory Registration) Order [1][2].

**Conditions to meet:**

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Applicable Standard** | Identify the relevant Indian Standard (e.g., IS 16102 Part 1 for Self‑Ballasted LED Lamps, IS 10322 Part 5 for Fixed General Purpose LED Luminaries, etc.) [3] |
| **Compulsory Registration** | Register the product under the CRS (Scheme‑II) on the CRS portal [6] |

**What to do today:**
1. Visit the CRS registration portal <https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/registration-page.do> and check the exact IS number for your LED bulb model.
2. Gather test reports from a BIS‑recognised lab for that IS.

**Next step:** Register your LED bulbs on the CRS portal and prepare the required test reports and documentation. [4]

## 3. My jeweller sold me gold without HUID. What can I do?

*intent=problem_solving · role=consumer · goal: Resolve issue with gold lacking HUID · standalone: "What can I do if my jeweller sold me gold without a HUID?" · answer by groq:openai/gpt-oss-120b (understand by groq:openai/gpt-oss-20b) · 12.9s · 3 sources · check: 0 removed, 4 re-cited*

**Problem‑solving steps**

1. **Verify that the article truly lacks a HUID** – Open the BIS CARE app, use the “Verify HUID” feature and enter the six‑digit alphanumeric number shown on the jewellery; if no HUID appears, the piece is not hallmarked correctly [1][2].

2. **Raise a formal complaint with BIS** – Contact BIS (via the consumer‑complaint portal or the BIS CARE app) and submit the bill/invoice, details of the jeweller and the gold article [3].

3. **BIS will investigate** – BIS officials will draw a random sample of the article and send it to a BIS‑recognised laboratory for testing [3]. If the test shows a shortage in purity, BIS will determine compensation as per Rule 49 of the BIS Rules 2018 [1].

4. **Compensation calculation** – The compensation amount is **twice the monetary loss** calculated on the basis of the purity shortage for the weight sold, plus testing charges [1].

5. **Possible outcomes**
   * **If the sample passes** – BIS may allow normal operation of the jeweller’s certificate to continue. [3]
   * **If the sample fails** – BIS may initiate cancellation of the jeweller’s registration under Section 7 of the Hallmarking Regulations [3].

6. **If the jeweller does not respond or redress within the stipulated time (90 days)** – BIS will proceed with cancellation of the jeweller’s registration [3].

**What you should do today**

1. Open the BIS CARE app and confirm the absence of a HUID.
2. Gather your purchase invoice, any photographs of the jewellery and the jeweller’s details.
3. File a complaint through the BIS consumer‑complaint portal <https://www.bis.gov.in/consumer-overview/online-complaint-registration/?lang=en> (or via the BIS CARE app).

Next step: Submit your complaint with the supporting documents to BIS now.

## 4. Difference between ISI mark and CRS?

*intent=compare · role=consumer · goal: understand the difference between the ISI mark and the CRS · standalone: "What is the difference between the ISI mark and the CRS?" · answer by groq:openai/gpt-oss-120b (understand by groq:openai/gpt-oss-20b) · 21.4s · 7 sources · check: 1 removed, 4 re-cited*

CRS (Scheme‑II) Registration** [1]

| Aspect | ISI Mark (Scheme‑I) | CRS – “Standard Mark” (Scheme‑II) |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Who uses it** | Manufacturers of non‑electronics products that need a licence to use the ISI (Standard) Mark. | Manufacturers of electronics and IT goods that are listed under the **Compulsory Registration Scheme** and declare conformity by self‑declaration. [1] |
| **Legal basis** | Governed by the BIS (Conformity Assessment) Regulations for Scheme‑I (licence‑based). | Governed by the **Electronics & Information Technology Goods (Requirements for Compulsory Registration) Order, 2021** and its 2026 amendment (QCO S.O. 1246 (E) dated 10 Mar 2026) [2][3][4][5]. |
| **Mark appearance** | ISI mark (the “ISI” logo) is affixed on the product. | “Standard Mark” (IS number + licence number) is affixed; colour scheme defined in Annexure‑III of the CRS Standard Mark Guidelines [6]. |
| **How conformity is shown** | Licence is issued after third‑party testing; the licence is valid for 2 years (renewable up to 5 years) [7]. | Self‑declaration of conformity; the Bureau may draw market samples for verification [7]. |
| **Application process** | Submit Form‑V, pay fees, undergo testing at a BIS‑recognised lab, receive licence. | Register online (CRS portal), upload self‑declaration, pay registration fee; no mandatory third‑party test unless the Bureau requests it [7]. |
| **Renewal / change of scope** | Renewal application 3 months before expiry; change of scope requires new test report [7]. | Licence is valid for 2 years; renewal follows the same online process; scope change may be requested with supporting test report [7]. |
| **Electronic labelling** | Not applicable. | e‑labelling optional for devices with integrated displays; physical label may also be used [6]. |

**Which one fits you?**
- If you are manufacturing **electronics/IT equipment** (e.g., digital cameras, mobile phones, UPS, copying machines) that appear in the compulsory registration list, you must use **CRS registration** and display the **Standard Mark** [2][3][4][5].

**Next step:** Register your electronic product on the CRS portal https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/registration-page.do.
